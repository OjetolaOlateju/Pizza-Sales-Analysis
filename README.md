# 🍕 Pizza Sales Analysis

An exploratory data analysis project using Microsoft Excel to examine pizza sales performance, identify customer purchasing patterns, and uncover insights that can support business decisions around inventory, product strategy and marketing.

![Pizza Sales Analysis](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/a790a947-2f59-4170-ba44-e9f79946c2dc)

## 1. Project Overview

This project explores pizza sales transactions to understand overall business performance, identify popular products and examine purchasing patterns across different days and times.

The objective was to turn raw sales data into meaningful business insights that could help a pizza business make more informed operational and marketing decisions.

## 2. Project Objectives

- Evaluate overall sales performance and key business metrics.
- Identify top-performing products based on sales volume and revenue.
- Examine purchasing patterns across different days and times.
- Analyse sales performance by pizza category and size.
- Identify periods of higher and lower sales activity.
- Develop practical recommendations from the findings.

## 3. Dataset Overview

The dataset contains pizza sales transaction records.

| Dataset Information | Details |
|---|---|
| Total records | 48,621 |
| Columns after cleaning | 14 |
| Data type | Sales transaction data |
| Analysis tool | Microsoft Excel |

### Key Dataset Fields

- Order ID: Unique transaction identifier.
- Pizza Name: Name of the pizza product.
- Pizza Category: Product category.
- Pizza Size: Size of the pizza.
- Quantity: Number of pizzas sold.
- Total Price: Sales amount per transaction line.
- Order Date and Time: Transaction date and time.

## 4. Tools and Techniques

**Tool Used**
- Microsoft Excel

**Techniques Applied**
- Data cleaning and preparation.
- Missing-value checks using `COUNTBLANK()`.
- Duplicate checks.
- Data type validation.
- Date and time extraction.
- Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA).
- Sales trend analysis.
- Data interpretation and business recommendations.

## 5. Data Cleaning and Preparation

Before conducting the analysis, the dataset was reviewed and prepared.

The following steps were completed:

- Checked for missing values using Excel's `COUNTBLANK()` function.
- Checked for duplicate records; no duplicates were found.
- Verified data types across numeric and text columns.
- Derived Day Name from the order date.
- Derived Day Time to support time-based analysis.

These steps helped prepare the dataset for consistent analysis.

## 6. Exploratory Data Analysis and Key Findings

### A. Overall Sales Performance

The analysis revealed the following key metrics:

| Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Total Sales Revenue | $817,860.05 |
| Average Pizza Price | $16 |
| Total Pizzas Sold | 49,574 |

### B. Product Performance

- **Classic Deluxe** recorded the highest sales volume.
- **Thai Chicken** generated the highest sales revenue.
- The **Classic** category recorded the strongest sales performance by category.
- **Large** was the most frequently ordered pizza size.

### C. Sales Trends and Customer Purchasing Patterns

- **Friday** recorded the highest sales activity by day.
- The **afternoon** was the peak ordering period.
- **January and November** recorded seasonal sales peaks.
- **September and December** recorded comparatively lower sales activity.

These findings provide insight into product preferences and variations in customer purchasing activity.

## 7. Business Recommendations

Based on the findings, the following actions could help improve business performance:

**Inventory Planning**
- Monitor demand for popular products, particularly Classic Deluxe.
- Align inventory planning with observed high-demand periods.

**Marketing and Promotions**
- Use the popularity of top-performing products to inform promotional campaigns.
- Consider targeted promotions during comparatively slower sales periods.

**Operational Planning**
- Prepare for increased ordering activity on Fridays.
- Review staffing and operational readiness during peak afternoon periods.

**Product Strategy**
- Use product and category performance to guide menu planning.
- Monitor sales volume and revenue separately when evaluating product performance.

## 8. Project Limitations

This analysis focuses on the sales information available in the dataset.

It does not include external factors such as marketing campaign performance, operating expenses, ingredient costs or customer demographics.

Consequently, revenue performance was evaluated, but product profitability was not established.

## 9. Conclusion

This project demonstrates how sales transaction data can be used to understand business performance and customer purchasing patterns.

Through data cleaning, exploratory analysis and interpretation of sales trends, I identified key products, peak ordering periods and seasonal variations.

The findings provide a practical basis for inventory planning, promotional decisions and operational improvements.
